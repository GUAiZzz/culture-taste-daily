## 一千多枚金圈，怎样戴回头上

### 中文

展柜会让珠宝看起来像一件件被抽离身体的微型雕塑。上海博物馆东馆新开的《珠宝：身体的变形》把提问转回来：首饰戴在哪里，怎样延伸、遮蔽或改变佩戴者？大都会艺术博物馆策展人 Melanie Holcomb 在2018年纽约原展的说明里说，若只把珠宝看成微型雕塑，就会漏掉它与人体的互动；不同文化也会把饰物放在身体不同位置。

11月18日至2027年5月5日，这个展览在上海博物馆东馆展出。上海市文旅局资料称，展览有大都会馆藏192件/套，上海博物馆另选二十余件中国古代珠宝；City News Service补充报道，展品中有2018年原展后新增入藏及近期修复的对象。六个主题从饰身、神性与超越，走到等级、吸引力和华美。这里，珠宝不只按材料和年代排列，它也被用来读信仰、权力、身份与欲望。

古埃及公主 Sithathoryunet 随葬的金发圈，让人看到这些饰物原来怎样戴。大都会馆藏记录说，她的墓里保存下1,251枚两种尺寸的金圈，原来的木盒和头发已腐朽；博物馆依照在另一处王室墓地发现的木头人头，把金圈重新编排在现代假发上。发圈从一段金属工艺，回到头发、身体和仪式的安排里。

这也提醒我们，博物馆展示身体并不容易。CFDA对2018年纽约展的评论指出，当时展厅没有把饰物放在活人身上，而需以照片和文字补足动作。那是纽约旧展的观察，不能直接代表上海现在的陈列；但它留下一把观看的尺子：眼前的陈列，能否让人想象首饰怎样改变佩戴者，而不只让人记住它有多珍贵？

上海版把二十余件中国古代珠宝摆进同一场展览。可以把埃及发圈和明代镶宝石金带饰放在一起看，比较两件东西各自戴在身体哪里。观看时，先问这件首饰戴在哪里、为何戴，再看它用了什么金属和宝石，或许更接近展览提出的问题：珠宝如何让身体被看见，也如何改变身体。

### English

In a display case, jewelry can look like miniature sculpture detached from the body. Jewelry: The Body Transformed turns the question back to the wearer: where is an ornament placed, and how does it extend, conceal or alter the body? Melanie Holcomb, a curator at The Met, made this point when the exhibition first appeared in New York in 2018: treating jewelry only as miniature sculpture misses its interaction with the human body, and cultures place adornment differently.

The exhibition opened at Shanghai Museum East on November 18, 2026, and runs through May 5, 2027. Shanghai's culture authority lists 192 works and sets from The Met and more than 20 ancient Chinese pieces selected by the Shanghai Museum. City News Service reports that the Shanghai selection also includes objects acquired since the 2018 presentation and others recently restored. Six sections move from adornment and divinity to transcendence, status, attraction and visual splendor. Jewelry here becomes a way to read belief, power, identity and desire as well as materials and dates.

A set of gold wig rings belonging to Sithathoryunet makes the body visible. The Met records 1,251 rings in two sizes recovered from her Middle Kingdom tomb after the wooden boxes and hair had decomposed. They were arranged on a modern wig using the form of a wooden head excavated at another royal cemetery. The rings return from metalwork to hair, body and ritual.

Museum display can still make that body difficult to see. In its review of the 2018 New York installation, CFDA noted that no ornament was shown on a living person; photographs and text had to supply movement. That describes the earlier galleries, not the Shanghai presentation. It leaves a useful viewing question: does the current display let you imagine how an ornament changed its wearer, or mainly how precious it is?

Shanghai’s selection places more than twenty ancient Chinese pieces in the same exhibition. Compare the Egyptian wig rings with the gold Ming-dynasty belt ornament, and trace where each one sat on the body. Ask first where an ornament was worn and why; then look at its metal or stone. That sequence comes closer to the exhibition's central question: how jewelry makes the body visible, and how it changes the body.

## 一张宣纸，被刺出数十万枚针孔

### 中文

11 月 19 日，REVELATIONS CHINA 2026 在上海展览中心开幕，公众展期到 23 日。上海市的活动页把它列入上海艺术品交易周，介绍展会将进入十多个城市场馆；开幕次日，看看新闻报道现场汇集了 42 个国家、200 多位艺术家和近 2,000 件作品。它既是一场展览，也自称是让工艺创作者接触收藏家和行业买家的市场平台。

趋势单元「与古为徒，以技为艺」邀请 15 位中国艺术家，付小桐带来《371,067 孔》和《215,670 孔》：她用针在手工宣纸上反复刺孔，让纸面密度形成山水和肌理。艺术家长期以针孔数量为作品命名。哈佛艺术博物馆也在 2025 年购藏了她 2023 年的《158,760 Pinpricks》。白兔美术馆介绍另一件早期作品耗时一年完成，描绘藏区的尕朵觉沃神山。这里的手艺不只是复刻水墨或刺绣，而是把时间、材料和针的动作变成作品本身。

展会也把这种转换放进更大的语境：南京作为“荣誉城市”，展位呈现云锦、绒花和十竹斋笺谱等传统工艺；现场同时有陶瓷、金属、纤维、木作和新材料。REVELATIONS 的巴黎母展强调它的经济功能，2025 年称 55% 的参展者实现了商业目标；上海场 20 日的论坛也直接讨论当代工艺需要怎样的市场平台。但巴黎的结果不能代替上海的销售数据，主办方设定的平台目标也不等于艺术家已经获得订单。

这场展览值得继续看的，是它能否让传统工艺不只停在“保护”和“展示”：创作者能不能把手艺发展成新的表达，并找到持续的观众与委托。现场有新的材料关系，也有市场承诺；真正的答案，还要看展会结束以后，作品和创作者能否继续被看见。

### English

REVELATIONS CHINA 2026 opened at the Shanghai Exhibition Centre on 19 November, with public viewing through the 23rd. Shanghai’s event listing places it within Shanghai Art Trade Week and says its Craft & City programme extends to more than ten venues. The day after the opening, Knews reported that the fair brought together more than 200 artists from 42 countries and nearly 2,000 works. It is an exhibition, but also describes itself as a market platform connecting craft makers with collectors and industry buyers.

The question I find most useful is not how many objects are on display, but how craft moves from “traditional technique” into contemporary work. The Trends section, *Learning from the Ancients, Using Technique as Art*, features fifteen Chinese artists. Fu Xiaotong presents *371,067 Pinpricks* and *215,670 Pinpricks*: she repeatedly pierces handmade Xuan paper so that the density of holes forms landscape and texture. The counts are not merely a promotional device—she has long named works by their number of pinpricks, and Harvard Art Museums acquired her 2023 work *158,760 Pinpricks* in 2025. The White Rabbit Gallery describes an earlier work that took a year to make and depicts the sacred mountain Gaduojuewo in Tibet. Here, craft is not just a reprise of ink painting or embroidery; time, material and the act of piercing become the work itself.

The fair places this transformation in a wider setting. Nanjing, the “City of Honour,” presents brocade, velvet flowers and Shizhuzhai printed manuals, while the event spans ceramics, metal, fibre, wood and new materials. The Paris parent biennial emphasizes its economic role and reported that 55% of exhibitors achieved their commercial goals in 2025. A 20 November forum in Shanghai also asked what kind of market platform contemporary craft needs. Paris results cannot stand in for Shanghai sales, and the organizer’s ambition does not prove that artists have received orders.

What is worth following is whether the exhibition lets craft move beyond “preservation” and “display”: can makers turn technique into new forms of expression and find a lasting audience and commissions? The show brings new material relationships together with a market promise. The answer will depend on whether the works and makers remain visible after the fair closes.

## Hasselt 让旧院落重新向城市开放

### 中文

WAF 2026 首日（11 月 18 日），“Creative Re-use”类别将比利时 Hasselt Beguinage 列为获奖项目。这座 18 世纪围合院落曾是贝居安女性社群的居所，中央教堂在 1944 年遭轰炸后留下废墟。如今，场地容纳 Hasselt University 建筑学院，也作为市中心公共绿地向学生、居民和访客开放。

战损教堂的遗迹留在现场，浅水池标出已消失建筑的轮廓，排水后可以成为活动场地；新开的圆形通道让人从街道进入原本内向的院落；一座新塔则重新把这处场地带回城市天际线。旧屋恢复一部分住宅尺度，容纳工作室、展览和讲座空间。

这次再利用也经过了公共讨论。Architecture Today 报道，建筑专业学生曾发起请愿，反对把场地交给私人开发；在没有私人投标者进入后，Hasselt University 提出把这里改为校园。项目最后由林堡省、Hasselt 市和大学合作推动。这个过程说明，遗产的下一种用途并非纯粹的设计题，谁能使用它同样是设计的一部分。

现在可以穿过庭园，在废墟边停留，也可以来上课、看展或参加活动。院落每天怎样被使用，比它在获奖照片里怎样被观看，更能解释这次改造的价值。贝居安女性社群的历史与现在的大学、访客空间并不相同；新功能没有延续她们原来的生活方式，却保留了场地可共同使用的部分。现有资料确认它向学生、居民和访客开放，但没有给出完整开放时段，因此不宜写成随时都能进入。

### English

On the first day of the 2026 World Architecture Festival, November 18, the Beguinage of Hasselt was named a winner in the Creative Re-use category. The 18th-century enclosed complex once housed a community of Beguine women. Its central church was left in ruins after being bombed in 1944. Today, the site is home to Hasselt University’s Faculty of Architecture and serves as a public green space in the city centre for students, residents, and visitors.

The restoration does not reconstruct the damaged church as it once stood. A shallow pool traces the outline of the vanished building and can be drained for events. A new circular opening lets people enter the formerly inward-looking garden from the street, while a new tower brings the site back onto the city skyline. The old houses have regained some of their domestic scale and now accommodate studios, exhibitions, and lectures.

The reuse also followed a public debate. Architecture Today reports that architecture students petitioned against private development. After no private bidders entered, Hasselt University proposed turning the site into a campus. The project was ultimately advanced through cooperation among the Province of Limburg, the City of Hasselt, and the university. The process shows that an old site’s next use is not only a design question; who gets to use it is part of the design too.

My reading is that the project’s main achievement is not turning the site into a monument to look at from a distance, but letting it take part in city life again: people can cross the garden, pause by the ruin, study, see an exhibition, or attend an event. The history of the Beguine community differs from the university and visitor uses now in place. The new programme does not continue the women’s former way of life, but it retains a shared part of the site. Available sources confirm access for students, residents, and visitors, but do not provide full opening hours, so it should not be described as open at all times.

## 一段没有图像的故事，最后成了照片

### 中文

11 月 14 日至 22 日，摄影师川谷光平 Kohei Kawatani 在东京藏前 Hender Scheme 的 Sukima 22.0 展出了《Stereoscope》。他把缺少固定图像的故事交给生成式 AI，将得到的图像转成立体数据，再用半透明材料做出物件，用相机拍下。观众看到的照片，经过了文字、生成图、数据和实体物件几道转换。

这道转换链也划出了照片能证明什么。它记录的是一件由文字解释而来、再被制造出来的物件；它不能单独证明原故事真实发生，也不是那段文字唯一正确的图像。这是从展览所述流程得出的判断：每一步都留下一个可见结果，也把前一步的内容重新组织了一次。

“Stereoscope”让人想到立体镜：两张略有差异的平面影像被双眼合看，才产生纵深。那种深度是观看方式带来的，不会替影像里的故事作证。川谷这次没有被报道为使用立体镜展示作品；标题更像是在提示，我们如何透过媒介看见一个被加工过的世界。历史上的立体照片也曾把异地风景带进家庭观看，影像因此既扩大了观看范围，也携带拍摄者和传播者的取舍。

这条线索和川谷此前的实践相连。2024 年，他曾把 AI 生成的参考图与据此拍摄的照片并置，让作品边界和图像来源变得可讨论；关于《Tofu-Knife》的报道则记录了他如何让真实物件看起来像仿制品。到了《Stereoscope》，不妨逐层追问：文字留下了什么，生成图改写了什么，物件与照片又分别让我们相信了什么？官网记录了这次展览的制作过程。

### English

From 14 to 22 November, photographer Kohei Kawatani presented *Stereoscope* at Hender Scheme’s Sukima 22.0 gallery in Kuramae, Tokyo. The exhibition begins with stories that have no fixed images: text is passed to image-generation AI, converted into 3D data, output as objects in translucent material, and finally photographed. The image a visitor sees has passed through several translations: text, generated image, data and physical object.

That chain also sets a limit on what the photograph can prove. It records an object made from an interpretation of text; by itself, it cannot prove that the original story happened, or that this is the story’s one correct image. This is an inference from the process described by the exhibition: each step leaves a visible result while reorganizing what came before.

The title *Stereoscope* recalls the optical device through which two slightly different flat images are viewed together to produce depth. That depth comes from a way of seeing; it does not verify the story inside an image. Kawatani has not been reported as displaying the work through a stereoscope viewer. The title instead invites a question about how we see a world mediated by images. Historical stereo photographs also brought distant places into domestic viewing, widening what viewers could see while carrying the choices of photographers and distributors.

This connects with Kawatani’s earlier work. In 2024, he placed an AI-generated reference image alongside a photograph made from it, making the boundary and source of an image open to discussion. Reporting on *Tofu-Knife* describes how he made real objects look like imitations. At *Stereoscope*, viewers can follow each step and ask: what remains from the text, what does the generated image change, and what do the object and photograph each lead us to believe? The exhibition page documents the process.

## Rashid Johnson 回到芝加哥，旧作也换了读法

### 中文
